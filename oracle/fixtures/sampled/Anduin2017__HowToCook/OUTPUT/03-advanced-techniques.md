# Advanced Techniques

Once you've cooked through a reasonable slice of the [Recipe Index](02-recipe-index.md)
and the fundamentals in [Kitchen Setup & Fundamentals](01-kitchen-setup.md)
feel automatic, these are the topics that separate "can follow a recipe"
from "can improvise one":

- **Auxiliary-Ingredient Technique** — how small supporting ingredients
  (aromatics, thickeners, acids, garnishes) are chosen and dosed to round
  out a dish, rather than treated as optional extras.
- **Advanced Terminology** — the vocabulary professional and semi-professional
  recipes use (cooking-wine deglazing, velvet-coating meat before stir-frying,
  "hot pan, cold oil," and similar shorthand) so that more advanced sources
  stop being opaque.
- **Making Sugar Color (Tang Se)** — the technique of caramelizing sugar in
  oil or water to produce the reddish-brown color and depth of flavor behind
  most red-braised dishes; distinct from just adding soy sauce for color.
- **Judging Oil Temperature** — reading visual and behavioral cues (smoke,
  ripple patterns, how a test ingredient reacts when dropped in) to hit a
  target oil temperature without a thermometer, since most stir-fry and
  deep-fry recipes specify temperature by feel rather than by degree.

These are deliberately kept separate from the main recipe index: they are
reusable judgment calls that apply across many dishes, not steps that belong
to any one recipe.
