# Kitchen Setup & Fundamentals

Before working through the recipe index, read the topics below. They are the
"standard library" that every recipe is written against — recipes will say
"blanch the vegetables" or "marinate for 20 minutes" without re-explaining
what that means each time, the same way a function doesn't re-explain what
its dependencies do.

## Environment

- **Kitchen Preparation** — the baseline set of tools, cookware, and pantry
  staples worth having before you start cooking regularly, so a recipe never
  stalls on "I don't own the thing this step needs."
- **How to Wash Dishes** — an often-skipped topic that determines how much
  the rest of cooking feels like a chore; doing it efficiently and in the
  right order (least dirty to most dirty, or soaking what needs to soak
  immediately) matters more than it sounds like it should.
- **How to Decide What to Eat Now** — a decision procedure for the recurring
  problem of picking a dish given what's in the fridge, how much time you
  have, and how many people you're feeding, instead of re-deciding from
  scratch every time.

## Appliances

- **Pressure Cooker** — how it cuts cooking time for tough cuts of meat,
  beans, and stocks, and the safety behavior specific to it.
- **Air Fryer** — what it's actually good for (crisping surfaces with little
  oil) and what it isn't a substitute for (deep frying, wet braises).
- **Microwave** — beyond reheating: steaming eggs, cooking rice or porridge,
  and other underused uses covered later in the recipe index.

## Core techniques

These recur across dozens of recipes, so they're documented once, here,
rather than duplicated in every dish that uses them.

- **Removing Off-Flavors (去腥)** — the general technique for neutralizing
  the "fishy" or "gamey" smell in meat, fish, and organ meat before cooking,
  independent of which specific dish you're making.
- **Food Safety** — safe handling and storage practices, and the failure
  modes (undercooking, cross-contamination, unsafe reuse of marinades) that
  matter most at home.
- **Learning to Blanch (焯水)** — briefly boiling an ingredient before the
  main cooking step, used to remove blood/impurities from meat or to
  pre-cook vegetables so a later stir-fry step is fast.
- **Learning Stir-Frying & Pan-Frying (炒与煎)** — high-heat, short-duration
  cooking in a wok or pan; the technique most recipes in the meat and
  vegetable categories are built around.
- **Learning Cold Tossed Dishes (凉拌)** — dishes assembled from cooked or
  raw ingredients plus a sauce, with no final heating step; mostly a
  sauce-balancing exercise (salt, acid, heat, sweetness) rather than a
  heat-control one.
- **Learning to Marinate (腌)** — how salt, acid, and time change texture and
  flavor before cooking, and how long is enough versus too long for
  different proteins.
- **Learning to Steam (蒸)** — gentle, moist heat, used for delicate
  proteins (fish), eggs, and buns; timing and doneness cues.
- **Learning to Boil (煮)** — the default method for staples like rice,
  noodles, and eggs, and the timing/doneness signals for each.

Once these are familiar, most recipes in the [Recipe Index](02-recipe-index.md)
reduce to "combine these ingredients using techniques you already know."
